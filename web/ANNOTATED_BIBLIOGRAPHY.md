Later-wave arrivals sample normalized 6-, 36-, and 124-year gaps, preserving short, medium, and long clustered spacing around the selected mean. Each wave also draws latent beta severity before institution-level binomial losses. The sparse gap bootstrap and beta concentration are authored conventions, not fitted forecasts; Hawkes self-excitation remains unimplemented. The count-only transition combines beta-binomial wave losses, fixed external fragments, and binomial completed splits. It does not identify winners, parents, lineages, internal politics, or partial autonomy. Institutions remain exchangeable within each wave. This controls how strongly economic strength and systemic stress alter consolidation, qualifying-event frequency, and internal fracture. The coefficients are transparent scenario choices, not estimates made by Dalio or fitted from history. A zero setting disables the economics layer. Four independent Latin-hypercube ensembles propagate transparent parameter distributions through the seeded binomial transition model. Non-reaching times remain censored. Transformed bell curves respect parameter bounds; external fragments use a zero-inflated count distribution. T₅ is the first modeled year with at most five independent top-level Institutions. Boundary comparisons lock the starting field at 225. Two years is the proven lower bound under current clamps; 5,000 years is an attained display-horizon boundary, not a natural maximum. Forty definition specifications and four leave-one-wave-out estimates test calibration fragility. Seven models are compared in six chronological test blocks. The target is COW membership exit, not permanent subjugation or Apex emergence. The baseline uses 244 dated state-system membership spells from COW v2024. Membership exits are observed events, but their causes have not yet been coded as consolidation, fragmentation, dissolution, or interruption. The hazard is a benchmark, not validation of the full model. Phases advance only when required data and tests exist. A blocked phase is an intentional result that prevents unsupported analysis.
