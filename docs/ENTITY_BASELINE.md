# Entity Baseline

**Baseline date:** October 6, 2026  
**Total modeled institutions:** 225

## Composition

```text
193 UN Member States
+ 2 UN Observer States
+ 30 revenue-driven technology corporations
= 225 modeled institutional agents
```

The country-level count follows the United Nations' presentation of 195 flags: 193 Member States plus the Holy See and State of Palestine.

## Corporate selection method

The corporate baseline is revenue-led rather than market-cap-led. Ranks 1–25 follow the 2025 Fortune Global 500-derived technology revenue table retrieved October 6, 2026. That table lists technology companies with annual revenue above approximately US$50 billion. Five additional large technology firms—HP Inc., Intel, Broadcom, Salesforce, and SAP—extend the interactive set to 30 using recent annual-revenue scale.

Because fiscal years, currency conversion, acquisitions, and sector definitions vary, ranks and values are approximate and time-sensitive. Revenue selects entities for the model but does not determine their political power, survival probability, or consolidation weight.

## Model interpretation

Corporations overlap with countries rather than replacing them. They are treated as transnational institutional agents because they possess persistent identity, leadership, resources, internal departments, strategies, and cross-border influence. This relaxes the paper's strict sovereign hierarchy and allows corporate-state, corporate-corporate, and mixed-bloc consolidation in the abstract model.

The visualization now retains one structural node per named institution while separately ranking economic influence. Countries use latest available World Bank GDP in current USD; corporations use approximate annual revenue. Country growth uses an approximately ten-year nominal-USD GDP CAGR, while company growth uses listed-origin GDP growth as a proxy. GDP and revenue are not accounting-equivalent and the combined ranking double-counts activity, so it does not determine political legitimacy, military power, or conflict outcomes. Holy See, Nauru, and North Korea remain explicitly unmeasured in the cached GDP panel.
