# Airline Consolidation as a Proxy Reference

**Date:** October 7, 2026  
**Status:** Corporate consolidation reference; not a sovereign-state calibration

## What the proxy can estimate

Under the model's decisive-event assumption:

```text
P(consolidation | decisive consolidation event) = 1
```

The airline reference is therefore not used to estimate whether consolidation occurs after a decisive event. It can inform:

- system-level consolidation event frequency;
- the relationship between financial pressure and merger activity;
- movement from many firms toward a concentrated market;
- winner/loser integration timing; and
- a fast regulated-corporate reference regime.

## Observed reference

The U.S. Government Accountability Office reported that four major airline mergers occurred beginning in 2007 and that approximately 85% of U.S. passengers flew on four domestic airlines in 2013.

A minimal system-event estimate is:

```text
4 major mergers / 6 years ≈ 0.667 major mergers per year
```

This is a small-sample market-level rate, not a stable universal hazard.

## Why airlines are not equivalent to states

Airlines operate within:

- one legal and antitrust system;
- common airports, air-traffic systems, and safety regulation;
- comparable services and route networks;
- bankruptcy and acquisition law;
- an external authority capable of approving or blocking mergers; and
- strong scale, network, fuel, labor, and capital pressures.

Sovereign Institutions generally lack an external merger authority and have different identity, coercion, legitimacy, territorial, and legal constraints.

## Event-granularity comparison

Starting with 225 Institutions:

### Current batch-consolidation model

The production model currently removes a fraction of Institutions at each qualifying system event:

```text
8 qualifying cycles
≈ 1,333 structural years
≈ 1,365 adoption-adjusted years
```

### Pairwise winner/loser events

If each decisive event has exactly one winner and one loser:

```text
required events = 225 - 1 = 224
```

At the current qualified sovereign interval:

```text
ordinary interval = 25 years
qualifying share = 15%
qualified interval = 25 / 0.15 = 166.7 years

224 × 166.7 ≈ 37,333 structural years
≈ 37,375 years including a simple adoption delay
```

This serial calculation is also unrealistic because it allows only one global pairwise event at a time.

### Airline-rate reference

Applying the U.S. airline system rate mechanically:

```text
224 / 0.667 ≈ 336 structural years
≈ 378 years including a simple adoption delay
```

This is a corporate-speed reference, not a world forecast.

## Main conclusion

The order-of-magnitude difference means event granularity and rate scaling matter more than fine-tuning many other parameters.

A stronger event model should use:

```text
λ_total(t) = Σ eligible institution-pair hazards
```

with parallel events, network eligibility, type-specific rates, and winner probabilities. Airline data can contribute to the corporate hazard prior; COW/UCDP data should inform state conflict rates separately.

Use airline consolidation to define a fast corporate reference scenario and plausible event-rate range. Do not substitute its rate for sovereign Institutions or describe it as validation of the current batch-consolidation equation.
